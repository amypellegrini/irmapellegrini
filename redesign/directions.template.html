Catalogue

The site as a monograph of forty years. A margin rail carries the dates, captions carry materials and provenance, and the works index reads as a proper catalogue. Best if the archive is the thing worth showing.

Irma Pellegrini

Wood carver · Córdoba · Neuquén · London
Chronology 1980s — Argentina 1987 — Neuquén 1993 — Córdoba 1995 — Pinamar 1997 — Buenos Aires 2001 — London 2007 — Greenwich 2012 — Taipei

Irma made her first wood carvings at the beginning of the eighties in Argentina, looking for a way of expression. Developing techniques of her own, she made progress in the knowledge of the wood and how to work with it, whilst facing more and more exigent aesthetic objectives.

In 1987 she became known in the city of Neuquén, showing her pictures at a shop in the centre. Given the impact of the exhibition and the demands from the public, she began teaching children and adults the fruit of those introspective years. Her techniques spread directly and indirectly — some of her disciples still teach them in schools and workshops today.

In her pictures she uses only wood's natural colour and grain, achieving this from the rests of sawmills, the forest ground, and the banks of lakes and streams. In 1993 she showed at the XI Córdoba International Handicrafts Fair, where she took first prize in the carved wood category along with two special mentions.

In 2001 Irma introduced her work in the United Kingdom, exhibiting at Camden Lock, Green Park, Chelsea, Highgate, the Red Ban Gallery of King's Lynn and, in 2007, the Greenwich Artists Collective. She continues to work on new shows and exhibitions.

Series · 80 movements

Tribute to Valentine

A limited collection of eighty movements in four different forms, for the priest who was killed for marrying a couple in a place and time where it was forbidden. No one knows what happened to the couple later.

Following the pulse of life, we honour Valentine by being present, open and perceptive to our own partners.

Series · Continuing

Leaves

These leaves come from the parks and trails. All are important and unique; they once stood here like us, and every spring they return. The figures are made with simple pieces of wood from cuts in carpentry.

It is an artistic expression that reflects us in our daily lives. Whatever we do, there are many times when we feel like a leaf in the wind.
